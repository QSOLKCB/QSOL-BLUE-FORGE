# Code of Ethics

QSOL-BLUE-FORGE exists to improve defensive security.

## Authorization

Adversarial testing must be authorized, simulated, synthetic, performed against systems the operator is permitted to test, or based on legitimate defensive evidence.

The project does not authorize activity merely because a technical capability exists.

## Minimum necessary intervention

Defensive action should be proportionate to the verified security requirement.

For equivalent security outcomes, prefer the smallest intervention that preserves the invariant and legitimate service behavior.

Broader containment is appropriate when evidence requires it, but it should be deliberate and documented.

## Deception outward, truth inward

Defensive deception may be used inside authorized environments to shape hostile behavior toward low-authority, instrumented surfaces.

Defenders must receive truthful records of:

- what was observed;
- what was inferred;
- what was simulated;
- what was verified;
- what was a decoy;
- what remains unknown.

Deception must not be used to fabricate confidence, evidence, identity, or attribution.

## No retaliation

BLUE-FORGE must not become a hack-back or retaliation framework.

Do not use the project to:

- damage remote systems;
- deploy malware to third parties;
- steal unrelated data;
- expose real production secrets as bait;
- interfere with systems outside authorized defensive scope;
- punish or retaliate against suspected attackers.

## Evidence integrity

Preserve evidence separately from interpretation.

A model assessment, scanner finding, analyst hypothesis, or consensus statement must remain labeled as such until an independent verification predicate is satisfied.

## Human consequences

When a defensive action could materially affect legitimate users, production availability, privacy, or access, prefer review and scoped containment over unnecessary broad disruption.

## Responsible disclosure

When BLUE-FORGE contributes to discovery of a vulnerability in third-party software or infrastructure, use responsible disclosure practices and avoid public release of details that unnecessarily increase exploitation risk before remediation is reasonably available.

## Governing principle

**Shape the terrain. Prove the defence. Do not become the threat you are trying to contain.**
